## the course

MUS 602, Post-Tonality and Beyond, is a graduate research-and-analysis seminar in the M.A. in Music, taught in the spring. Students take it after MUS 601, and it begins chronologically where 601 ends. My analytical lens (symmetry, interval cycles, form as process) is a through-line of the seminar.

## who owns what

- `README.md`: the course overview, public-facing.
- `meta/`: planning and conventions, mine and Claude's. Markdown anywhere in the repo is mine and Claude's, except the READMEs. The repo is public, so all of it is readable at the repo and at the Pages URL.
- `assets/style.css` and the fonts are copied from mus601 when the first page is built, and the two copies are updated together from then on.

## assessment

Assessment covers the iterative research paper (proposal, annotated bibliography, draft, revision, final), analytical assignments, seminar participation, and a conference-style presentation. Grades are A to F on the 0 to 4 grade-point scheme in mus601's [meta/assessment-scheme.md](https://github.com/csuebmusic/mus601/blob/main/meta/assessment-scheme.md).

## research and bibliography

The research module runs alongside the paper sequence above. The paper is a full research paper developed and revised across the term, and its bibliography is its scholarly context. Students build and annotate a set of scholarly sources, on the order of eight to twelve, weigh competing analytical frameworks and take a position, and read sources against each other to place their work in the existing scholarly conversation. The annotated bibliography uses the 601 research skills at a larger scale.
